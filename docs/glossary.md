# Glossary

Terms this codebase uses without defining, written for someone who reads the code
but not the domain. Each definition says what the thing *is in this codebase*,
not what the term means in general — where those differ, the difference is the
interesting part.

Clinical terms are used in their ordinary senses. [PHQ-9 and GAD-7](#screening-instrument)
are the exception: they are named in code, so they are defined here.

## AI and provenance

**`AiResult<T>`**
The return type of every method on `AIService`. `{ data: T; source: "model" | "fallback"; degradedReason?: AiDegradedReason }`. There is no method that returns a bare string, so the compiler enforces that every AI-backed feature can say where its content came from. Defined in `server/src/services/ai.service.ts:40`; the reasoning is in
[ADR-0002](adr/0002-ai-provenance.md).

**Provenance**
The `source` flag on an `AiResult`, plus `degradedReason` when it is `fallback`.
It answers "was this written by a model or generated by a template, and if the
latter, why?" Provenance is persisted wherever the text outlives the response —
`PreSessionData.briefingSource` — because a clinical briefing is sometimes read
days after it was written.

**Degraded reason**
The second half of provenance: *why* a fallback was produced.
`not_configured` (no key), `circuit-open` (upstream known down),
`timeout`, `error`, `empty` (model returned nothing), `malformed` (model returned
something unusable). Six values because they are six different operational
problems with the same user-visible result.

**Circuit breaker**
`lib/circuitBreaker.ts`. Opens after `AI_CIRCUIT_THRESHOLD` consecutive failures
(5), stays open for `AI_CIRCUIT_COOLDOWN_MS` (60 s), then allows one half-open
probe. A failed probe re-arms rather than closing on a single sample. It exists
so requests do not each pay a 20-second timeout against a dependency already
known to be down.

**Fallback**
Deterministic local output used when the model is unavailable. A supported
outcome, not a failure — it is what keeps the product usable during a provider
outage. `fallback` text is written so it is honest about being a fallback; the
clinical briefing, for instance, is prefixed `[Automated summary — written by the
platform, not by a clinician]` and quotes nothing.

**The one exception to disclosure**
Crisis and escalation replies in the support assistant are **not** labelled as
canned. They are fixed safety instructions carrying emergency numbers, and
marking them as automated would make the one message that must not look
automated look automated.

## Clinical safety

**Disclosure**
A clinically significant signal that a patient has expressed thoughts of
self-harm. Three sources produce one: a non-zero answer to PHQ-9 item 9, the SOS
button, and crisis phrasing in a patient-to-clinician message. A disclosure
becomes a `RiskAlert` row and is escalated to the assigned clinician.

**Risk queue**
The clinician-facing triage worklist at `/api/wellness/risk-alerts`. It defaults
to **unresolved**, not unacknowledged. Severity sorts ahead of acknowledgement, so
a still-urgent disclosure is never pushed below a routine one merely because
somebody glanced at it. A match is never an action: nothing auto-escalates to an
emergency service.

**Acknowledged vs resolved**
Two distinct states, and the distinction is the whole point of the worklist.
`acknowledgedAt` means a human has seen it; `resolvedAt` means it is dealt with.
Resolving implies acknowledging, and the audit entry records that it did. A
resolved row stays in the table.

**Assigned vs notified**
`RiskAlert.assignedDoctorUserId` is whose queue it sits in — the clinician
actually treating the patient. `notifiedDoctorUserId` is who was told, which may
be a clinician, may be up to five admins, and is often nobody at all. An alert
raised at 23:00 pages the on-call admin but belongs to the treating clinician.

**Keyword match (as a triage label)**
The queue's own wording for a free-text crisis hit: *"This is a keyword match, not
a clinical assessment - it is a prompt for a clinician to read the message and
decide."* It is on every `reason` string from the free-text path and it is the
reason over-matching is acceptable.

**Screening instrument**
A validated self-report questionnaire producing a score, used for monitoring
over time rather than diagnosis. Two are implemented: **PHQ-9** (Patient Health
Questionnaire, 9 items, out of 27) for depressive symptoms and **GAD-7**
(Generalized Anxiety Disorder scale, 7 items, out of 21) for anxiety. Item 9 of
the PHQ-9 asks directly about thoughts of self-harm or death.

Two things follow from "instrument" that are easy to miss: the score is *not* a
diagnosis and the product never presents it as one; and each instrument has its
own denominator, which the briefing prompt used to get wrong by printing "N/21 or
/27" for both.

**Worklist**
A list of items awaiting human action, with enough state to express a backlog.
A worklist that removes an item the moment it is *seen* is not a worklist. Used
in this repository to mean the risk queue specifically, and referred to as "the
only triage worklist a clinician works" — all four disclosure sources land in one
table so there is one place to look.

**Purpose claim**
The `purpose` field in every JWT this service mints — `access`, `refresh`,
`2fa-pending` or `ws-ticket`. Verification always asserts the *expected*
purpose, so a token minted for one flow cannot verify as another. A pending 2FA
ticket presented as an access token fails because the purposes differ, and
because `TWO_FACTOR_SECRET` must differ from `JWT_SECRET`.

**Reassessment**
Completing a screening instrument again. The value is the trajectory rather than
any single score, which is why `GET /api/wellness/assessments/trajectory` returns
a series with a change-since-previous and an explicit insufficient-data threshold
rather than a lone number.

## Realtime and video

**ws-ticket**
A 30-second, single-purpose JWT a browser hands to the realtime service to open a
websocket. It exists because the handshake cannot carry an `Authorization`
header and the `accessToken` cookie is host-only for the API origin — passing the
15-minute access token as a query parameter instead would put a live session
credential into every proxy access log on the path. Minted only after the session
has been re-validated against the database, so a banned user or an unfinished
2FA challenge cannot obtain one.

**Room seed**
`Appointment.roomSeed`, the per-appointment random component of the video room
name (`mindease-{appointmentId}-{roomSeed}`). It is persisted rather than
generated per request because the two participants join **independently** and
must arrive at the same room. Created when the appointment is confirmed and
cleared on reschedule, because a new slot is a new session and must not inherit
the old room.

## Money

**Idempotency key**
A client-supplied unique string on a write that makes a retry safe. `Appointment`
has one: `POST /api/appointments/book` accepts `idempotencyKey`, checks it before
creating, and the column is `@unique`. The same mechanism is the *proposed* fix
for the checkout race that the payments path does not have — see
[ADR-0003](adr/0003-payment-abstraction.md).

**Package purchase**
An entitlement to a block of therapy sessions. Created `pending`, moved to
`active` when a verified payment callback lands. Legitimate only if it has a
`paidAt` or an explicit `grantedByUserId` from an administrator — nothing else
grants sessions.

**Session credit**
`User.sessionCredits`: one free session, earned by referring someone who
completes a session. Claimed with a conditional `updateMany` so two concurrent
bookings cannot both spend it, and refunded on cancellation
(`Appointment.creditApplied`). Distinct from a package purchase: a credit is a
count, not a block.

**Claim** (the thing that gets "held")
`node-cron` jobs claim work by writing a sentinel timestamp with a conditional
`updateMany` — `reminderSentAt`, `checkinSentAt`, `lastMoodNudgeAt`,
`lastDeclineNudgeAt` — and only the runner whose `updateMany` returns
`count === 1` proceeds. That single-row conditional write is the entire
multi-replica safety mechanism; there is no queue and no lock that decides who
runs. A claim is a hold on work, and the term "hold" is deliberately not used for
it, because these columns are not a reservation that can be inspected or released
— they are the record that the work was done.

The claim is taken **after** the eligibility check, and that ordering is
load-bearing. It was the other way round once, so a session whose calendar date
fell inside the reminder horizon but whose start time did not burned
`reminderSentAt` and was skipped on every future run: the patient was never
reminded and nothing reported the loss.

## Two-factor

**Backup code**
A single-use recovery code for when the authenticator is unavailable. Stored as a
JSON array of Argon2 hashes in `User.backupCodes`, so the plaintext is shown once
at enrolment and never persisted. Verified against Argon2, and consumed on use.

**TOTP replay guard**
`User.lastTotpStep`, the highest TOTP step already accepted. A TOTP code is a
function of a 30-second time step, and the ±1 step tolerance exists to absorb
latency — which means the same code is technically valid three times. Recording
the highest accepted step means a replayed code inside the window is rejected.

## Scheduling

**Slot**
A `ConsultationSlot`: one bookable window for one clinician, identified by
`(doctorId, date, startTime, endTime)` — unique, so an exact duplicate is a
database error. The uniqueness constraint is not an overlap check: an application-
level overlap check still exists, and the constraint is what catches the case two
concurrent requests both pass it.

**Away window**
`Doctor.awayUntil`, meaning "unavailable from now through the end of that day". It
is stored at local midnight and compared as `awayUntil <= now` so the bound is the
end of the away day. The comparison was originally `>= awayUntil`, which hid the
slots *before* the away date and kept offering the ones inside the window — the
exact opposite of what it says. An away clinician is excluded from the directory
and from slot results.

**Rebook**
`GET /api/appointments/:id/rebook-options` — the alternatives offered when a
session needs rescheduling, derived from what is already known about that
appointment and clinician rather than being a generic calendar. Distinct from
*reschedule*, which moves an appointment to a chosen slot and resets it to
`pending`; rebook is the read that tells you what you can move it to.

**Follow-up**
A session a clinician suggests at the end of a consultation, as a `FollowUp` row
with a proposed date and status `pending | accepted | declined | expired`. It is
a proposal, not a booking: the patient accepts it, and only then does it become
an appointment.

## What is not here

Terms that are ordinary enough not to define: booking, slot, session,
consultation, triage, notification, webhook, middleware, ORM, migration,
idempotent. If one of those turns out to mean something specific in this
codebase, it should be added here.
