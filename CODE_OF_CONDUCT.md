# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We want participation in this project to be a harassment-free experience for
everyone, regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, gender identity
and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status,
nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and
orientation.

## Our standards

Behaviour that contributes to a positive environment:

- Being curious about the reasoning rather than the conclusion. Most of this
  codebase looks unusual in places, and usually there is a good reason
  documented in a comment.
- Assuming the person who wrote it knew something you did not, and asking.
- Being direct about a problem without being personal about the person.
- Respecting that other people have different levels of familiarity with
  mental-health software, clinical practice, or Indonesian.

Behaviour that is not acceptable:

- Sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention of any kind.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political
  attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing others' private information without explicit permission.
- Clinical advice. If you are a clinician reading this for the code, the
  software is not a validated instrument and should not be used to make a
  decision about a real person without independent clinical judgement.
- Reporting a vulnerability publicly instead of through
  [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces — the repository, issues, pull requests,
discussions — and when representing the project in public.

## Enforcement

Report conduct to the maintainer through a private channel: GitHub's
[private vulnerability reporting](https://github.com/davinakmalyasha/MindEase/security/advisories/new)
works for this, or a direct message. Reports are handled confidentially.

Responses will be proportionate: a correction, a warning, a temporary ban, or a
permanent ban. Reports that are good-faith and believed to be in error are
handled as such.
